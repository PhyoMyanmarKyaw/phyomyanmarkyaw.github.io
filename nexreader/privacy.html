NexReader Privacy Policy

Effective October 16, 2026

In short: NexReader has no accounts and never sees your documents. It collects anonymous usage statistics and crash reports so the app can be improved, and you can switch that off.

This policy explains how NexReader ("the app"), a Markdown reader made by Phyo Myanmar Kyaw ("I", "me"), handles information. It applies to the app on iPhone, iPad and Mac.

Your documents stay with you

The files you open or import are stored on your device, in the app's own folder. They are not uploaded to me or to anyone else. NexReader has no account, no sign-in and no server of its own.

What is collected

NexReader uses two services from Google's Firebase:

  • Google Analytics for Firebase records anonymous usage: that the app was opened, which features were used (for example, that a document was opened, or that Find was used), an approximate region derived from your network address, and your device model and system version.
  • Firebase Crashlytics records crash reports: what the app was doing when it failed, together with your device model and system version.

Both use an identifier created for this installation of the app. It is not your name, your Apple ID or your advertising identifier, and it is reset if you delete and reinstall the app. Crash reports are kept for 90 days, and analytics data for at most 14 months.

What is never collected

  • The contents of your documents
  • Their titles, file names or folder names
  • What you search for
  • Your name, email address, contacts, photos or precise location
  • The advertising identifier (the app does not include it)

How it is used

Only to understand how the app is used and to find and fix crashes. It is not used for advertising, is not sold, and is not combined with information from other apps or websites. NexReader contains no advertising and does not track you.

Turning it off

Open Reading Settings (the “Aa” button in the library), scroll to Privacy, and switch off Share Usage & Crash Data. Collection stops and stays off.

Wi‑Fi Transfer

On iPhone and iPad, when you choose “Receive over Wi‑Fi”, NexReader starts a small web server on your device so a computer on the same network can send it files. It runs only while that screen is open, is protected by a code that changes each time, is reachable only on your local network, and sends nothing to the internet.

Images in documents

If a document refers to an image by web address, NexReader downloads that image to show it. The website hosting the image will see the request, as it would in a web browser.

Google's role

The analytics and crash data described above are processed by Google on my behalf. Google's own practices are described in the Google Privacy Policy and in Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Selling data

I do not sell your personal information.

Children

NexReader is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
